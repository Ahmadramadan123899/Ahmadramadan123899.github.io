Now · M2 research project · 2026–2027

Can your heartbeat tell an expert from a novice?

ECG · lead I ♥ 72 bpm
RR intervals → HRV featuresnovice ⟷ expert

Multimodal analysis of ECG/HRV data for expertise assessment during assembly tasks

M2 research project · Université de Lille · egocentric perspective · EgoLOAD dataset

When someone assembles an object, their body reacts to how hard the task feels for them. The idea of this project is to use heart activity (ECG) and heart-rate variability (HRV), from an egocentric (first-person) perspective, to estimate how expert a person is, by comparing people in pairs.

  1. 01
    Literature review

    Expertise assessment from physiological signals: what has been measured, how, and what worked.

  2. 02
    Concepts

    Understanding the key concepts and how expertise relates to physiological measurements such as HRV.

  3. 03
    EgoLOAD dataset

    Getting familiar with the data: assembly tasks recorded from an egocentric perspective, with ECG.

  4. 04
    Models

    Statistical analyses and deep learning models that classify expertise levels through pairwise comparisons.

Research · CRIStAL lab · 2025–2026

Facial Expression Recognition from Videos

A model that watches a short face video and says which emotion it shows, by following 68 face points over time and reading the skin around each point.

Code on GitHub ↗
5datasets (3 lab, 2 in-the-wild)
68facial landmarks per frame, 16 frames
96.62%CK+ accuracy (paper: 98.78%)
+7.84points on AFEW with my changes

The method of Zhao et al. (IEEE TAC, 2023) had no public code. In the M1 research project I rebuilt it from scratch and validated it on CK+ (92.98%). During the internship I extended it to five datasets, investigated why it does much worse on real-life clips from movies and TV shows, and tried new ways for the model to look at the face.

On lab data, the rebuild matches the paper closely. On in-the-wild data it started far behind; my dual-pathway encoder closed a good part of the gap on AFEW.

Video · 16 frames→ 68 face points (2D-FAN)
+ patch around each point→ 3 graph branches
+ ResNet-18 (FER+)→ Fusion→ GCN + spatial & temporal transformers→ Emotion
  • The face points form a graph: points that belong together (corners of an eye, outline of the mouth) are connected.
  • For each point: its position relative to the nose, its position inside its own face part, and how much it moved since the last frame.
  • Graph convolutions share information between neighbours; transformers let every point attend to every other point and every frame to every other frame.
  • An attention step learns which face parts and which frames matter most (the mouth for a smile, the peak of the expression).
  • Training: cross-entropy + center loss. Fine-tuning the ResNet patch encoder end-to-end alone raised AFEW from 29.77% to 38.64%.

Vélib' Bike-Share Data Pipeline & Forecasting

An end-to-end pipeline on live Paris bike-sharing data: ingestion every 5 minutes, warehouse modelling with tests, orchestration, dashboards and a one-hour forecast.

Code on GitHub ↗
~1,500stations, polled every 5 minutes
17dbt data tests
2Airflow DAGs (5 min + hourly)
−19%forecast error at the morning rush

Raw station status is saved as replayable files, then loaded into PostgreSQL by an idempotent Python loader, so a run can be replayed without duplicates. dbt turns it into a star schema plus ML features, Airflow runs everything, and pytest + GitHub Actions keep it honest.

One real data problem: some stations reported impossible capacities. I fixed them with a 95th-percentile rule on observed bikes and docks.

A LightGBM model predicts the number of available bikes one hour ahead, retrained every hour by Airflow. I also tested hourly Paris weather (Open-Meteo) as extra features: honest result, no gain yet.

Motion-to-Text Retrieval 🏆 1st of 9 teams

Given a 3D motion of two people interacting (a handshake, a push, a hug…), find the sentence that describes it among 32 candidates.

Code on GitHub ↗
0.703private score, 1st / 9
0.896public score, 2nd / 9
256-dshared motion–text space
4 dayscompetition length

A CLIP-style model: two encoders map motions and sentences into the same space, and candidates are ranked by cosine similarity. The private half of the test set was harder for every team; ours dropped the least, which took us from 2nd to 1st.

Motion
frames × 384→ + velocity, acceleration
+ person-to-person distances→ BiGRU + BiLSTM
attention pooling→ cosine similarity← MPNet
pretrained, fine-tuned
  • Symmetric contrastive loss (InfoNCE) with a learnable temperature, batches of 64.
  • Augmentation: random rotation around the vertical axis and swapping the two people (“one person… the other”).
  • Dropout 0.3, AdamW with weight decay 0.05, cosine schedule, gradient clipping; normalization statistics from the training split only.
